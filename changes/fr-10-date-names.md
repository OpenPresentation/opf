FR-10 (part 2, a declared drawing change): the month and weekday names of a date are the language's where the date is drawn. `MMMM`, `MMM`, `EEEE` and `EEE` in a date variable's `format` and in a header or footer `dateFormat` print the names of the slide's declared language, else the deck's (English when none is declared or the table has no data for the language, so a deck with no declared language, and an English one, draws exactly as before); a month in a pattern with a day token is in its form after a day ("5 марта"), without one in its stand-alone form ("март 2026"), a language that counts its months prints the whole label ("3月"), and the digits are the numerals table's. A date variable with no `format` uses the language's long date pattern (`d. MMMM yyyy` in German, `d MMMM yyyy` in French and British English, `yyyy年M月d日` in Japanese); US English and a deck with no language keep `MMMM d, yyyy`. A deck's header and footer variables take the language of each slide they are drawn on: a slide in another language than the deck's gets a resolved copy of the deck's furniture in its own. The names come from a generated table (`packages/javascript/src/date-name-data.ts`, 88 languages, ICU 78.3 and CLDR 48.0, 12.7 KB gzipped, read by `scripts/build-date-names.mjs`; `ay`, `ber-Latn` and `ctg` have no data and draw English); the entries that draw dates carry it, and their size budgets in `scripts/catalog-free-budget.json` were raised by its measured cost. `formatFurnitureDate(iso, pattern, language?)` gains the language, and core exports `dateNamesOf` and `longDatePattern`. In PowerPoint a current date with month or weekday names stays a native English field only in a deck whose names are English, and is fixed text in another language, so the file shows the words OPF drew. The drawing changes only for a deck that declares a language other than English (or `en-GB` and the like for a date variable with no format) and draws a date with names or without a format; no gallery record draws differently. See `docs/templates-and-variables.md`, "Dates in other languages".
