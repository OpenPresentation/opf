Breaking (0.35, output-changing for decks in Arabic script or Hebrew that declare no language): a deck with no `language` is no longer always laid out as English. A slide whose letters are mostly Arabic-script or Hebrew is resolved as Arabic (`ar`) or Hebrew (`he`): it gets the script's default fonts and a right-to-left layout, and the exported PPTX carries right-to-left paragraphs, the language tag on the runs, the theme's script fonts and mirrored layouts. The preview and the PPTX are those of the same deck with the language declared, apart from the declaration itself (the SVG's `lang` attributes, `dc:language`), and importing the PPTX gives back a deck with no language. Before, such a deck was drawn left to right with a fallback face, and PowerPoint ordered its mixed lines differently from the preview. Each slide is read on its own from the text it draws (more than half of its letters decide), so an Arabic slide in an English deck is right to left and an English slide with an Arabic phrase is not. A declared language always wins (deck, slide or option); `"language": "en-US"` keeps the old layout. Only right-to-left scripts are read from the text. `resolveScriptFonts()` reports `languageSource: "text"` for such a slide, and `opf/missing-language` names the slides that were read as Arabic or Hebrew.
