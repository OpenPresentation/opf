A chart can shade ranges of its value axis behind the data: `chart.bands`, one to six `{ to, label?, color? }` (`ChartBand`), each running from the previous band's `to` (the first from the axis minimum) to its own, in the scheme's neutrals (the text color mixed into the surface in OKLab to a contrast of 1.75, 1.45 and 1.25 against it, lowest band first) or a light tint of a scheme color. Bar, column, line and area charts and their stacked forms draw them, the last `to` extends an automatic axis, and labeled bands come first in the legend. A new chart type, `bullet` (with its gallery record), draws one thin bar per category against a target tick (`mapping.target`) over its bands. Core sorts and clamps the bands and computes their colors and the bullet's plot for every engine (`chartBands`, `chartBandRanges`, `chartBandFill`, `bulletPlot`; the gauge reuses them). The PowerPoint export writes the bands as a stacked chart group on the primary axes and the data on deleted secondary axes of the same scale, so a chart with bands has fixed value-axis bounds in PowerPoint, and the bullet's target as a scatter series with fixed Y error bars on the measure's axes; the band series are workbook columns after the data, and import restores the authored bands, target and type while the caches are unchanged, else reads the bands and targets PowerPoint holds. The editor has a Bands section in the chart inspector, offers Bullet in the chart type picker (taking a column named like a target as `mapping.target`) and marks the target column in the data grid. New warnings: `chart-bands-order` and `chart-bands-unsupported`.
