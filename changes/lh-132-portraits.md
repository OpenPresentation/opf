LH-132 (output-changing for teams and testimonials with pictures): portraits at card size. A slide whose body is only people, each a group of a picture followed by a name (a list of one entry whose description holds the role and a line, or a short text), draws each person as one card with a portrait instead of a small avatar over the name: 2 to 4 people in one row with the picture across the top of the card at its width (beside the text when the card is too wide, as for two people), 5 or 6 people in a grid with the picture beside the text at 40% of the card's width (35% or 30% where the wider column sets the text larger) on the logical start side. A picture beside the text is drawn only where the column keeps every name on one line and holds at least 20 characters a line; otherwise a top portrait is tried, and failing that the slide keeps its arrangement without portraits exactly (more people, or a card too narrow, too). No picture ends smaller than the slide draws it without the rule. The names, roles and lines take the largest type-scale size that fits beside a square portrait (down to 21 px, then beside a 4:3 one) and the portrait takes the room left, at most 4:5 tall, never flatter than 4:3; the cards are equal and centered in their cells. The pictures cover their frame around the authored focus, or the upper third, in a frame rounded as a card is (an authored shape other than a circle is kept), so they never distort. A testimonial's photo becomes a tall portrait at the start of a quote at least 640 px wide (26% of its width, narrower where the text needs the room), its text beside it and centered against it, instead of the circle beside the attribution. Both are the guarded rule `portraits`: no name, role, line or quote part is smaller than with the avatar (the growth passes choose their step with the avatar, then draw the portrait at that step). New format key `portrait`: on a person's picture (`"top"`, `"side"` or `"avatar"`; the first person's value applies to the whole team) and on a quote (`"side"` or `"avatar"`) overrides the choice; validate reports it on a payload that is not an image (`opf/image-option-unsupported-payload`). The preview crops a quote portrait around its `focus` and PowerPoint writes it as a native `roundRect` picture with the matching `adj` and an `a:srcRect` crop around the same focus; raster portraits are not re-encoded. PPTX export records an authored quote `portrait` in the quote's provenance and an image block's in its treatment, so import restores them. The editor's image treatments accept `portrait` (`setImageTreatment`, `IMAGE_PORTRAITS`).
