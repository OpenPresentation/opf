The PowerPoint importer reads chart caches that Keynote rewrote, and one unreadable chart no longer costs the whole deck (opf#1477, opf#1478). A cache whose `c:ptCount` is lower than its highest point index (Keynote writes the number of points present, so every chart with a gap, and every 0.36.0 highlight chart, came back that way) now reads by the points' own indexes: the extent is the larger of the count and the highest index plus one, the missing points are gaps, and one `chart-cache-repaired` diagnostic names the chart; the same rule applies to chartex dimensions. A multi-level category cache (Keynote splits labels such as "Month: place" at the colon into an inner level with the part after it and the whole label, and an outer level with the part before it) reads as one label per category, from the cached whole label or else the levels joined outer to inner with ": ", with one `chart-categories-flattened` diagnostic. A chart whose cache still cannot be read (a duplicate point index, a malformed count, competing caches, an out-of-range number, a cache over the import limits) imports as the text placeholder `PowerPoint chart: <name>` (or the classic frame of a chartex chart) with one `chart-unreadable` diagnostic at `slides.N.charts.M` (`reason` is the former error's code, `location` its path), and the other slides import as before. Before, the first such chart threw `invalid-chart-cache` or `unsupported-chart-cache` and ended the import. An unedited OPF export reads exactly as before. The three diagnostic codes have help text in `opf` (the reporter).
