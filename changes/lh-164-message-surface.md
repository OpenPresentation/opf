LH-164, LH-163 (output-changing): a slide whose whole body is one display message draws on the accent surface, as Claude Design draws a statement or a one-sentence slide. The body is one message when it is LH-09's `statement-message` (a text in the `statement` record) or `display-message` (a lone text of at most 25 words, in any script, with no citation, footnote or subtitle, in a layout of one body region and no picture area); a metric, a quote and any slide with a second block keep the plain surface. The surface is `accent1` in the deck's accent order, taken in the place of a layout variant under the same conditions (the slide's own `design` sets no variant or background and the deck and theme draw no fixed background), only where the theme's accent carries the text at 4.5:1 and differs from the deck's own surface; a layout that names its own variant (`section`, `closing`) keeps it and the statement's `surface` gives way. A deck never shows two accent surfaces on consecutive slides (a message surface, a layout's accent or a slide's own accent variant before it), so the second keeps the plain surface and a run of message slides alternates. `resolveSlideContext(...).resolved.messageSurface` `{ kind, variant }` is the reason (`layoutVariant` and `roles` follow it, so the preview, the PowerPoint export with its `clrMapOvr`, the validator and the editor read it as they read a layout's variant), and `composeSlide` echoes it as `SlideComposition.surface`. A color the author set on a run of the message that does not read on the surface draws in the nearest one that does. LH-163: in a display message on a dark or accent surface, the bold words draw in a lighter tint of the primary that reads 4.5:1 there (hue and saturation kept) where the primary itself does not. The PowerPoint import drops the `variant` that the surface implies, so a round trip adds nothing to the document.
