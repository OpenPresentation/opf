LH-28, LH-29 and LH-20: three PPTX export options and one finding family.
    - `typefaces: "safe"` (`opf convert ... --typefaces safe`) writes each Latin text family as an Office-safe one and measures the text in that family, so every viewer breaks lines where the file does. Office-safe means the family ships with every Windows and macOS machine and has a metric-compatible open face; from the font policy table that is Arial, Courier New, Georgia and Times New Roman. A metric twin keeps its widths (Arimo becomes Arial); other families follow their font scheme's type (sans-serif becomes Arial, serif Georgia, monospace Courier New). Script, symbol, emoji and math families keep their names. Each replacement is reported as `typeface-replaced` (info). The default, `"selected"`, is unchanged. A default export now carries one `pptx/typeface-not-office-safe` info finding naming the families a viewer may substitute, and opf-pptx exports `typefacesNotOfficeSafe(bytes)`, `OFFICE_SAFE_TYPEFACES` and `officeSafeTypeface()`.
    - `as: "images"` (`--as images`) writes each slide as one full-bleed PNG picture drawn by opf-render, at `scale` 2 by default. It keeps the speaker notes, hidden flags and sections, and uses the slide's text as the picture's alternative text. It is a fidelity fallback with no editable text; the text, chart and record options are refused with it.
    - `export-lossy` (info, with `feature`) reports what the export draws only approximately: `svg-rasterized` (it replaces the `svg-image-rasterized` code and now also covers SVG picture bullets), `border-dot` (a dotted table border is PowerPoint's square dot), `pattern-preset` (`diagStripe` is `wdUpDiag`) and `image-animation` (an animated WebP becomes its first frame). The opf-export skill lists every mapping.
