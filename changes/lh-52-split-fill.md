LH-52, LH-71 and LH-86 (layout harvest; output-changing for automatic slides of two or three blocks that leave the body half empty, and for layout slides of two or three long paragraphs in cards side by side). A split is two or three blocks (no group, no caption) at the root of the body, and its fill is the share of the body they draw: the ink of text, lists, quotes, metrics and code; a table at most its natural width by the height of its rows; a timeline at its needed height; a picture or video at its frame; a chart in the largest 4:3 box of its cell. LH-52: a split that fills under 55% of the body no longer reads as a strip along the top. In a row (any mode), a code panel takes its whole cell with its code in the middle, other measured blocks sit in the middle of their cells (a card still fills its cell), and pictures, videos and charts are centered. In a column, the measured blocks keep their needed heights (a table no longer pushes its neighbor to the foot of the body) and the stack is centered. No block changes size. LH-71: in `auto` mode with no `columns` or `weights`, a split tries one row and one column besides the scored grid and keeps whichever fills the most, under three conditions. A row must give each block at least 120 px of width, a column must give each picture, chart and video at least 120 px of height, neither may overflow more, and neither may set its blocks smaller unless it removes an overflow. A table that wraps in a row now goes full width above its text, and texts alone keep their row. The explanation records the choice as the new decision reason `split-fill`. Explicit `row`, `column` and `grid` compositions are kept. LH-86: on a slide that draws cards, two or three paragraphs of at least 80 characters that a layout drew side by side (in regions of one row, or in one region's grid) are drawn one card per row, across the row. Every paragraph moves to the row's first region and the others collapse into it. A stacked column of long-text cards shares the region's height at the largest type-scale size where every row fits, with the gap closing to half when that sets the text larger, and each text centered in its card. When the texts would fill under 45% of the height, the cards hug their text and the stack is centered. The rows are kept only when their smallest body size is at least the side-by-side one, or they overflow less. Of the 360 composition goldens, 10 moved (two-column, three-column and pillars with 2, 3, 5 or 6 long blocks) and none gained a finding. Across the 844 corpus slides, 136 changed, findings stay at 539, no slide gained one, every block is still drawn, and no slide's smallest body size went down (none went up).
