LH wave 8 and shapes phase 1 integration (output-changing where the features meet). Shape blocks draw their effects: the preview wraps a shape's outline and its text in SH-03's SVG filters (the outer shadow and the reflection include the text, as PowerPoint's do) and the PowerPoint export writes SH-03's `a:effectLst` in the shape's `p:spPr` right after its line, with `{ color, lighten }` and `{ color, darken }` effect colors as theme colors with `a:lumMod` and `a:lumOff` and every length at the slide's scale (`ComposedShape.effectScale`); `examples/technical/shape-effects.opf.json` shows every effect and shadow preset. Every wave 8 composition rule (`heading-tracks`, `run-in`, `figure-row`, `note-band`, `icon-cards`, `text-tiles`, the stepper's drawn-size comparison and the message surface's display check) runs inside the one size guard of `composeSlide`, with LH-149's half-step discount. A figure-led list whose entries carry icons is LH-169's row of figures first and takes LH-165's icon cards where no text then reads smaller (the larger text wins, the cards on a tie; a numbered step's icon is still not drawn). The cards of a row share their label track, so a row's descriptions start on one line (LH-18). A band's text that opens with a short "Label:" draws the label bold inside the band, as n18's "Recommendation:" (LH-63 with LH-170). On the message surface, a run color written as `{ color, lighten }` or `{ color, darken }` is held to 4.5:1 as any other ColorRef is (LH-164 with SH-02). A mixed card on a dark or accent slide stops mixing towards the text where the text would read under 4.5:1 on it (`CARD_TEXT_MIN_CONTRAST`): on an accent slide (the section layout, an accent variant) icon cards, text tiles and the section's cards draw a little closer to the surface and their text reads 4.5:1. The engines and icon sets that load on demand (`ensureDiagrams`, `ensureShapes`, `ensureIcons`) share one preload, documented once in `docs/how-opf-works.md`. The image stroke's composed field and the opf-layout skill say `stroke`, not `border`.
