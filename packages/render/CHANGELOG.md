# Changelog

## Unreleased

## 0.32.0 (2026-11-04)

- Diagrams: a new `diagram` content kind (on slide roots, blocks and layout regions; a string of Mermaid source, or `{ source, syntax, alt }`) draws flowcharts, sequence, class, state, ER, gantt, pie, mind map, timeline, journey, C4, architecture and the other built-in Mermaid 12.1 families with [pele](https://pele.run), in the deck's own fonts and colors. Core lays each diagram out with the deck's measured fonts at the largest size that fits its block, never below the readability floor (a diagram that only fits smaller is drawn smaller and reports a `text-overflow` finding with advice), and centers it; node fills, outlines and data series follow the slide variant and the chart palette, so dark slides and color maps adapt on their own. `alt` is the text alternative (without it engines derive one from the diagram's labels), a source that cannot be parsed reports a finding with its line and column and draws a "Diagram unavailable" placeholder, and a diagram takes a `caption`. A fenced block whose info word is `mermaid` is a diagram in Markdown (` ```mermaid alt="What it shows" `), written back losslessly. The PPTX export writes a native SVG picture over a PNG fallback (crisp, with `alt` as its description, round-tripping through `fromPptx`); the nodes are not yet editable PowerPoint shapes. Limits: 20,000 characters of source and 500 edges. Node, Bun, Deno and edge runtimes load every diagram type with core; a browser bundle loads pele's core (about 12 KB gzipped) and each type the first time a deck draws one, through the new `#diagram-engine` import map: the renderer's browser fonts handle loads them in `ensure` (and lists them in `pending` as `diagram:<type>`), `toPptx` loads them itself, and other hosts call `ensureDiagrams(presentation)` from `@openpresentation/opf/composition`. New fixtures: `examples/technical/diagrams.opf.json` and `examples/markdown/diagrams.opf.md`.
- LH-01 (output-changing for slides on the section, statement, quote and closing layouts): a layout record can name a background variant in `design.variant`, either a variant or a value relative to the deck's own variant (`surface`: light2 on a light deck, dark2 on a dark one; `brand`: the brand dark, dark2, with light text; each takes the other slot where the deck is already on it). The default section draws on accent1, statement and quote on `surface` and closing on `brand`, so a dark deck keeps its tone. A layout's variant outranks the deck's and the theme's variant or background (it hides their background, and keeps the deck's accent order when it names none); the slide's own `design.variant` or `design.background` still wins. A variant's background can now be an accent (`accent1` to `accent6`, `ColorVariantSlot`): the accent is bg1 and bg2 and the text is the scheme's light family (light1, light2) or dark family (dark1, dark2), whichever reads better on it, with tx2 on tx1's slot where the family's tx2 is under 4.5:1. Core resolves it once (`resolveSlideContext` `resolved.roles` and `resolved.layoutVariant`, the validator's contrast checks, `composeSlide`'s background picture); the preview draws the variant's bg1 and the PPTX export writes the slide's `clrMapOvr` (`bg1="accent1"` for an accent) and imports it back as the variant its layout already gives. New on `/composition`: `layoutColorVariant(layout, deck)`, `layoutVariantApplies`, the `ColorBaseSlot`, `LayoutVariantRole` and `LayoutVariantDeck` types; `colorMapFor` takes the color scheme as a second argument for accent variants. The preview no longer paints a theme-slot background that sits beside a variant at the same level (`{ "background": "light1", "variant": "dark1" }` drew light text on white): the slide is the variant's bg1, as core and the PPTX export already resolved it.
- LH-02 (output-changing for slides with text on a picture background): an automatic scrim under text on a picture background. When text sits on a picture and the background has no `overlay`, core adds a scrim in the slide's background color behind the text (`SlideComposition.backgroundImage.scrim`): a gradient from the bottom, top or side edge the text is near, or a band across centered text that is solid only over the text and fades out over one and a half times its height (every fade eased), one each for the body, the header and the footer, at the lowest opacity (in hundredths) that keeps the slide's text at 4.5:1 over any picture, the gray ramp from black to white. A host that can read pixels passes `pictureLuminance` (a compose option, and an option of `toSvg` and `toPptx`), built with the new core helper `luminanceGrid` from decoded pixels, and the scrim is sized for the picture under the text instead. The preview draws it as an SVG linear gradient and the PPTX export as one native rectangle with a `bg1` gradient fill whose stops carry alpha; it replaces RR-96's 40% scrim on a picture that names its `tone`, and import reads it back as no content. An overlay of the author's always wins, and the new `overlay: false` on an image background turns the scrim off (the editor's background panel has a "No automatic scrim" box for it). `opf/text-on-image` notes the scrim, and the contrast rules now read a slide's own design (its background, tone or variant), which they had dropped.
- LH-06 and LH-68 (layout harvest; output-changing): one emphasis surface per slide, and scorecard metrics sized from their tiles.

- **`emphasis` on a block or group** (schema, core, render, PPTX). `emphasis: true` draws the block or group on the slide's emphasis surface: a card in its cell, with no card of its own for anything drawn on it. Its colors come from the deck's own scheme slots through the LH-01 variant machinery (new core `emphasisSurface`): on a light slide the brand dark (dark2, then dark1, then the accents), on a dark slide the accents in the slide's order, then light1, the first whose text reaches 4.5:1 on its fill and whose fill reaches 3:1 against the slide. Every text role on the surface (text, secondary text, primary, secondary, accent) under 4.5:1 is drawn in the surface's text color. A slide has one surface: the first `emphasis: true` in reading order takes it, any other is drawn without it and reported (composition diagnostic `emphasis-ignored`). Composition reports the surface as `SlideComposition.emphasis` (`path`, `box`, `radius`, `shape` card, band or pill, `source`) and marks every item on it `emphasis: true`; the preview and the PPTX export draw it from there (PPTX: a tagged `OPF card` frame with no line, imported as decoration), and `validate` measures the contrast of those items against the surface.
- **Automatic emphasis** (no authoring). On a slide without an authored emphasis, a short last text block (60 characters or fewer) after blocks that are all longer (any other kind, or text over 60 characters) is drawn as a full-width band at the foot of the body, and one of 30 characters or fewer after a lone long text as a pill one gap under that text's last line, at the start of its column (at the foot of the body when there is no room there). The body above composes in what is left, with a layout or without. **No block loses size to them**: the slide is also composed without the band or pill (what it drew before), the band or pill text starts at least at the size the block fitted at there, and the band or pill is kept only when it leaves fewer findings, or as many and no body block (text, list, any visible part of a quote, code panel, metric or timeline, a table's smallest cell) smaller. `emphasis: false` keeps a block out; a slide with an arrangement of its own (a `composition` mode other than auto, weights or columns) takes neither. Across the 844 slides of the LH-27 corpus, 8 take a foot band and 10 a pill; no slide gains a finding and no block gets a smaller size. Of the 360 composition goldens, 7 cases take a foot band (timeline, metrics, chart, chart-beside, table, table-beside and code with 2 short blocks), none gained a finding.
- **Metric tiles** (layout schema, core, gallery). A layout region's new `metrics: "tile"` lays each metric in it out from its whole cell: the label first as an eyebrow, the value under it asking for a quarter of the cell's height (between its 76 px role size and twice it, new `layoutMetric` option `tile`), a stacked unit under the value, and the description, delta and trend at the foot of the cell, start-aligned. Tiles fill their cards (no hugging) and siblings still share their sizes (LH-43). A tile never sets a role smaller than the metric fitted at as a stack (new `layoutMetric` option `minimumSizes`, from a stacked composition of the slide); where tiles would still cost a size or a finding, the slide keeps its stacks. The gallery `scorecard` layout's `cards` region uses it; its 12 composition golden cases moved, and across the corpus 18 metric values grow (76 to about 114 px) and none shrinks.
- LH-07: numbered step badges and a connector for process and agenda, and short lists as an arrowed flow. A layout region's new `sequence` key (`steps` or `numbered`) lists the steps of a sequence. In a `steps` region, two or more text or list blocks draw under numbered badges: an accent circle 1.75 times the step text across, its numeral at the step text size in the scheme color readable on the accent, on one connector line through the badge centers. A one-entry numbered list gives its badge its number and draws as a heading over its description. A lone list draws its entries beside badges down a connector, and a lone list of 3 to 6 entries of one or two words is a row of chips joined by arrows. In a `numbered` region (an agenda, which runs in time), every list draws beside badges, numbered or not: an unnumbered list is counted in arabic as drawing only, continuing across the region's lists (unless one of them cannot take badges), and the document and its PPTX import keep it unnumbered. Steps keep the text size they have without the badges (a guarded rule, `step-badges`), and right-to-left decks run from the right. Core reports the drawing as `SlideComposition.steps`, list entries as `ListEntryLayout.badge` (`fitList` option `badges`) and adds `listNumberLabel`, `STEP_BADGE`, `STEP_BADGE_GAP` and the `STEP_FLOW_*` limits. The preview draws them, and the PPTX export writes native shapes: an ellipse holding its numeral, a line, rounded rectangles and `rightArrow` or `leftArrow` presets, each tagged `OPF_STEPS_V1`. Import consumes those shapes and gives a list its numbering back. The gallery's `process` layout uses `steps`; `agenda` and `agenda-split` use `numbered`.
- Layout harvest LH-08, LH-32 and LH-50 (output-changing: slides with a tag, with a short label before a longer text, or with a list of short labels with descriptions draw differently):
    - **Ruled rows (LH-08).** A new design key, `design.listStyle` (`stack` or `rows`; deck, slide, theme and layout), draws a list as ruled rows: a hairline between two first-level entries (`ListFit.rules`, 1 reference pixel in the scheme's border neutral), 0.8 em between rows and no bullet (numbers stay). When every entry has a description, the labels are bold and, when the widest fits 40% of the width on one line, sit in a column beside their descriptions, first baselines aligned (the label column at the right in a right-to-left list). Without the key, a list of two or more entries that each pair a short label with a description is drawn as rows when that sets no block smaller (the guarded rule `list-rows`). The preview draws the hairlines as SVG lines and the PPTX export as native line shapes.
    - **The tag as an eyebrow (LH-32).** The slide tag is bold and, where its script has case, drawn in capitals with 0.08 em of tracking (`TextStyle.caps` and `tracking`, `EYEBROW_TRACKING`, `eyebrowCaps`), at 22 reference px (`EYEBROW_TAG_SIZE`, up from 16) in the primary color as before. Core measures the capitals and the spacing; the preview draws them with `letter-spacing`, and PowerPoint gets the authored text with `cap="all"` and `spc`, so it stays editable and imports as written.
    - **A short label as the eyebrow of its text (LH-50).** A text block of 3 words or fewer with no full stop, right before a text at least twice as long, is drawn as that text's eyebrow in its cell (`ComposedItem.eyebrow`, `eyebrowLabel`, `isLabelText`) instead of a block of its own, at 0.6 of the paragraph's size from 20 to 24 px and under it (`eyebrowSize`); on a card the two share one card. The rule is guarded (`eyebrow`): the paragraphs keep at least their size.
- LH-113 and LH-115 (output-changing for slides whose texts name a chart's categories or a table's rows, for bar charts beside a table of the same keys and for a timeline beside a column chart of the same keys): shared emphasis and row alignment across charts, tables, lists and timelines. See `docs/content-payloads.md#highlighted-rows`, `docs/chart-options.md` and `docs/dynamic-composition.md#rows-shared-across-kinds-lh-115`.
    - **`table.highlight`.** `{ "rows": [...] }` names body rows by the text of their first cell, as `chart.highlight` names categories. The preview and the PPTX export draw them on an accent tint of the table surface (15% of the primary color, mixed less where the cell text would fall under 4.5:1; core `tableHighlightColor`, `TABLE_HIGHLIGHT_MIX`); a cell's own fill wins. The export writes the tint as the cells' fill and import restores the list (also when only some cells were edited, while the named rows keep their keys). A name no row has is the new validation error `table-highlight-unknown-name`.
    - **Focus from the whole slide.** When the title names no focus (LH-105), a chart's focus and a table's highlighted rows come from what the slide's texts, list entries, quotes and chart alts name together (core `slideFocusSources`, `chartSlideFocus`, `tableSlideFocus`, `focusHits`; `ComposedItem.tableHighlight`, `TableLayoutOptions.highlight`, `TableCellLayout.highlighted`). Conservative: stop words, ranges, quantities, enumerating lists, long paragraphs naming several keys and disagreeing sources name nothing; a table also matches a printed percent against its percent cells. An authored `highlight` or `colors` (or a body cell fill on a table) turns it off.
    - **Bars on a table's rows.** A horizontal bar chart beside a table whose keys are its categories in the same order centers each bar on its key's row (`ChartLayer.rows`, core `alignBarRows`), when the rows fit in the chart's plot band. The PPTX export writes the plot over the same rows; PowerPoint spaces categories evenly, so on rows of unequal height a bar sits off its key by at most half the difference.
    - **A timeline under its column chart.** An automatic slide of a timeline and a column chart whose `when` values are the chart's categories in order draws the chart above and the timeline as a band under the plot, one event under each column (right to left mirrored with the columns), when that reads as well as the slide without it.
- LH-131, LH-137 (output-changing for slides with a before and after or problem and solution pair, a marked pricing tier, a metric delta or a short list on a dark slide): composition finds the featured card of a pair or a set from content alone (core `featuredCells`: "after", "the solution" or a tier marked "recommended" or "most popular", in fifteen languages, never from ids) and draws it on the LH-06 emphasis surface (`emphasis.source: 'featured'`) with the other cells on plain cards; a before and after pair of metrics side by side gets an accent arrow between its cards (`emphasis.arrow`). A metric's delta draws as a pill with its trend arrow beside the value, or above it, where every field keeps its size (`layoutMetric` `deltaPill`, `MetricLayout.deltaPill`, core `metricPillFill`). A dark slide whose body is one short bulleted list draws its entries as check-marked tiles (`steps` form `tiles`, `ComposedStepBadge.check`, core `checkMarkPoints`). All three are guarded rules (`featured-card`, `delta-pill`, `check-tiles`), never cost a block its size, and an authored `emphasis: true` always wins. The preview and the PPTX export draw the arrow, pill, badges and check marks as native shapes (preset arrows, rounded rectangles, ellipses and a custom geometry), tagged and consumed on import.
- LH-133, LH-134 (output-changing for FAQ, glossary, takeaway and agenda slides): questions, terms and numbers drawn as Claude Design draws them, read from the content and never from a slide id. A list of questions with answers (`questionText`: a text ending in a question mark of its script, `?`, `？`, `؟` or `⸮`) is drawn as ruled rows, each question bold in a column of 40% of the width with its answer beside it (the guarded rule `pair-rows`); a region whose blocks are each one question with its answer (an FAQ written one block per question) stacks them as ruled rows across the region, the answers at one edge and at the list size (`ListFitOptions.rowLabels`), and six or more short questions keep the region's grid with a 3 px accent rule over each cell (`ListRule.accent`, `LIST_ACCENT_RULE_WIDTH`). The slide's lone list of terms with definitions, most of them one word (`pairEntry`, `TERM_WORDS`), is a glossary whose terms draw in the accent (`ListEntryLayout.accent`). A lone numbered list of two to five takeaways draws display numerals in the accent beside ruled rows (2.5 times the list size beside takeaways with a supporting line, `LIST_NUMERAL_SCALE`), and an agenda whose entries all have descriptions draws as numbered ruled rows ("01" at the list size, the description in a second column) instead of LH-07's badges (`ListFitOptions.numerals`, the guarded rule `numerals`, `ComposedStepBadge.numeral`). Ruled rows of any kind fill the body as LH-48 fills it: a lone ruled list grows in type-scale steps, then shares the leftover height evenly between its rows, at most 3 em apart (`ListFitOptions.spread`, `LIST_ROWS_SPREAD`), and stacked question blocks spread the same way; one rule decides between numerals, badges and plain rows (docs/dynamic-composition.md, "Numerals, badges or rows"), and LH-07's veto holds: the numbers are drawing, an unnumbered agenda imports unnumbered and an authored numbering comes back from each numeral's tag. The preview draws the accent terms, accent rules and numerals, and the PPTX export writes them as run colors, native lines and tagged text boxes at core's geometry; import reads none of them as content and now keeps two stacked lists the export named apart instead of merging them into one.
- LH-135, LH-136 (drawing changes for pricing tiers and one-word pillar titles only): a slide whose body is 2 to 5 groups of a labeled metric and a list or text (pricing tiers) draws each as one card of equal height in one row, with the tier name as the metric's eyebrow (`MetricLayout.eyebrow`, `MetricLayoutOptions.eyebrow`: bold capitals with tracking, in the eyebrow's color, as an editable run with `cap="all"` in PowerPoint), the price at display size on one line and the details from the top of the card (guarded rule `tier-cards`, `TIER_VALUE_SIZES`); a slide of 2 to 5 one-entry lists whose titles are each one word draws the word at display size over an accent rule without a card (guarded rule `pillar-words`, `fitList`'s `display` option, accent `ListFit.rules` with a `thickness`, a native line in PowerPoint). Both rules sit inside the size guard, so no block reads smaller than before except the label, which becomes an eyebrow at the tag's 22 px; other pillars and tiers keep their cards.
- LH-145 (output-changing for slides with text on a picture background): `measurePictures(deck, options?)` of `@openpresentation/opf-render` decodes the picture backgrounds of a deck (data URIs, `asset:` references and what a host `imageResolver` returns, each once, cached per resolved source) and returns the `pictureLuminance` sampler of the automatic scrim, so a dark photo under white text gets no scrim in the preview and the PPTX while a light one keeps it. The decoder loads lazily: sharp in Node (the optional peer, as for PNG output) and a canvas in the browser entries, which hold no Node module. `opf convert` and `convert()` measure for every format (a new `measurePictures: false` option sizes every scrim for any picture again; a picture that cannot be decoded is the info finding `render/picture-not-measured` and keeps the worst case), the editor's canvas measures in the page and redraws when a measurement arrives (`measurePictures: false` turns it off; a host's own `renderOptions.pictureLuminance` wins), `convert` of the editor measures too, and the Export menu hands `exportPptx` the sampler as `pictureLuminance`. `toPptx` declares the option it already read.
- LH-99: hosts that draw slide by slide show the export's continuation of an overfull slide (LH-90) without shifting a source slide index.
    - Core: `continuationPages(result, slideCount)` maps a `continueOverfull` result back to the source slides (`pages[i]` lists the output indices that draw source slide `i`).
    - opf-render: the player, the speaker view, `<opf-deck>` and its thumbnails show a continued slide's pages as steps after it, each the export's own page (the same `toSvg` call on the export's deck). Slide numbers, `goto(n)`, the `slide` attribute, number-then-Enter and `slidechange`'s `slide` and `index` keep addressing source slides; the new `page` and `pages` (on the element, the session, `currentSlide` and `slidechange`), the `startPage` option and the counter (`2 / 3 · 2/2`) name the page. Next and previous step through the pages. `toHtml` draws each page as its own figure. `continuation="off"` and `continuation: false` draw every slide as written. Decks without an overfull slide draw exactly as before.
    - opf-editor: the slide is edited as written; the thumbnail strip and the slide list draw a continued slide as a stack with its page count, the canvas shows "Exports as 2 slides" with a read-only preview of each page (`canvas.pages`, `canvas.previewPage`, `onPages`), and the presenter shows the pages as steps (`getPage`, `onPageChange`). `continuation: false` on `createOpfEditor`, the canvas, the strip and the presenter turns it off.

- LH-03 and LH-81 (output-changing for column, bar, line, area and combo charts): charts carry their own caption and legend, and small charts label their values.
    - **Small charts (LH-03).** A clustered column, bar or line chart of one or two series with eight or fewer points per series, whose labels fit beside each other, labels every mark with its value (outside the end of a column or bar, above a line point), drops the value axis, its gridlines and the gray chart panel, and, with one column or bar series, draws its last point in the series color and the others in a quieter tint of it (core `chartEmphasisColors`): the tint keeps 3:1 against the backdrop (WCAG 1.4.11) and 15 L* from the series color, and where the backdrop leaves no room for both every point keeps the series color. In PPTX the value axis is deleted (`c:delete val="1"`), the chart area has no fill and each quieter point is a `c:dPt`.
    - **Header row (LH-81).** A column, bar, line, area or combo chart draws a caption above the plot when it adds to the legend (one series: "Count by stage"; several series: only from a value axis title, "Revenue ($M) by quarter"; the category in sentence case, acronyms such as "Q1" and "FY27" kept; the series part alone outside English decks) and, for two to four series, its legend in that row or the next instead of at the side, so the plot keeps the chart's full width. In PPTX the caption is the chart title, and the title, the top legend and the plot area (`layoutTarget outer`) carry core's positions as `c:manualLayout`.
    - Composition decides both once per chart item: `ComposedChart.layer` (core `chartLayer`, `chartHeader`, `chartHeaderCaption`, `smallChart`, `chartLayerDataLabels`, on `/composition`), and the composed value axis is sized for the plot below the header. An explicit option wins: `dataLabels: false` keeps a small chart's value axis and draws no labels, a value axis title keeps the axis, `highlight` and `colors` keep their colors, a `legend` option keeps that legend and draws no header row, and a payload `caption` replaces the header caption. `fromPptx` does not write the default labels or the header legend back as options.
- LH-103 (output-changing for decks with a fixed background): a fixed background the author set beats a gallery layout's `design.variant`. When the deck or the theme draws a hex color, a solid whose color is a `var:` reference, a gradient, a pattern or a picture, the section, statement, quote and closing slides (and any layout that names a variant) keep that background, and their text follows its `tone` or luminance as on any other slide; the preview draws the deck's picture or color and the PPTX export writes no `clrMapOvr` or scheme background for them. A scheme-based deck background (none, a theme slot, a solid whose color is a scheme slot or role name such as `primary`, or a deck `variant`) still takes the layout's variant on top, and the slide's own `variant` or `background` still wins. A deck with both a fixed background and a `variant` keeps the background; a deck `variant` hides the theme's background, so the layout's variant applies there. New on `/composition`: `isFixedBackground(definition)` and `deckBackgroundIsFixed(deckDesign, themeBackground)`; `layoutVariantApplies(slideDesign, layout, deckDesign?, themeBackground?)` takes the deck design and the theme background.
- LH-105 (output-changing for every chart): chart defaults follow Stephen Few. Every explicit option still wins, and import writes no default back. See `docs/chart-options.md#defaults-follow-few`.
    - **No panel.** No chart draws its gray panel any more: it stands on its card or the slide background, against which its colors and text are judged. The PPTX chart, plot and chartex chart areas are unfilled.
    - **Soft series colors.** Series from the scheme are softened towards the backdrop (`softenChartPalette`, `chartPaletteForFill(..., { soften: true })`: HSL lightness 25% of the way to the backdrop's and saturation at 75%, less where contrast or the lightness step between neighbors needs it). The PPTX export writes them as the theme accent with `a:satMod`, `a:lumMod` and `a:lumOff` (`chartSoftColors`). An authored `colors` entry is unchanged.
    - **Emphasis from the slide title.** The series or categories the slide title (and subtitle) names, by name, month or printed value, keep their full-strength color and the rest take context grays, one per context series or slice, 11 L* apart (`chartTitleFocus`, `chartContextColors`, `ChartLayer.focus`): 3:1 against the backdrop, or a light 1.6:1 gray when every context mark carries its text (value labels, line-end names, slice names); `Intl.Collator` matching works in any language. Without a match, the small chart's last-point emphasis (LH-03, LH-95) applies only to categories ordered in time (`chartCategoriesInTime`: years, quarters, months, weeks, dates, numeric sequences, with `Intl` month names and time words of the deck language), and the emphasized point takes the full-strength color.
    - **Direct labels.** A line chart of two to four series names each series at its line end with its last value ("61 Enterprise NPS", bold for the series the title names) instead of a legend (`ChartLayer.endLabels`, `chartEndLabels`); a pie or one-series doughnut names its slices instead of a side legend (`ChartLayer.slices`, `chartSliceLabels`). Each falls back to its legend when the names do not fit. In PPTX: the last point's `c:dLbl` and one `c:dLbl` per slice.
    - **Fewer ticks.** `CHART_MAX_INTERVALS` is 6 (was 8) and `maxIntervalsFor` caps there (was 10); a value axis with room for three intervals is never tightened to one. The PPTX axes have no tick marks.
    - **Validator.** Two info findings: `opf/chart-dual-axis` (a combo chart with `secondaryAxis`) and `opf/chart-pie-many-slices` (a pie or doughnut of more than five slices). `opf/chart-color-only` and `opf/chart-colors-adjusted` judge colors against the slide background.
- LH-107 to LH-110 (output-changing for waterfalls, wide pies and doughnuts, stacked column and area charts, and charts whose title compares with a value or names a period): chart marks from the Claude Design chart comparison. Composition decides them on `chart.layer` (core `chart-marks.ts`); the preview and the PPTX export draw them; every explicit option still wins, and import writes no default back. See `docs/chart-options.md#chart-marks-waterfall-roles-value-lists-stacked-labels-reference-marks`.
    - **Waterfall roles (LH-107).** Totals (the inferred last total and the first bar it runs from) in the strong color (`dark2` when it reads on the backdrop, else the text color), gains in the accent, losses in the context gray (`chartWaterfallColors`, `waterfallRoles`); every bar labeled with its signed value (`+9.3`, `−7.4`; totals unsigned and bold, `waterfallLabelText`) when the labels fit, the value axis then dropped and the column name drawn as the caption; dashed connectors in the preview. PPTX (native chartex): role colors per point, the signed number format `"+"F;"−"F;F` (`waterfallSignedFormat`), bold total labels, a hidden value axis and the caption as `cx:title`; PowerPoint draws its own solid connectors (chartex has no line format for them). A waterfall with `colors` keeps its palette positions.
    - **Value list beside a pie (LH-108).** A pie or one-series doughnut of two to eight categories, without `legend` or `dataLabels`, lists its categories and values beside it (swatch, name, value right-aligned, the title's focus bold, the unit above) when the pie keeps its radius beside the list (80% of it when a slice is under 10% or the slice names would leave the pie); otherwise it keeps its slice names (`chartValueList`). PPTX: the pie in core's square (inner plot layout), no legend, the list as tagged native shapes.
    - **Stacked values and names (LH-109).** A stacked column or area chart without `dataLabels` or `legend` prints each segment value that fits inside it (not on a 100% chart, nor at an area's ends) and names its series beside the last column instead of a legend (`chartStack`, `placeStackNames`); the plot is pinned. PPTX: the chart's own center labels with one `c:delete` per value that does not fit, the names as tagged native shapes.
