# Compose and repair OPF layouts

Work on the supplied OPF document without silently rewriting or dropping content. Use the installed OPF schema to check available composition options; older package versions may not contain the current composition/pagination APIs.

<!-- glossary:start -->
**Words these skills use**

- **Furniture**: the parts that repeat on slides outside the content: header, footer, logo, slide number. You set them once on the deck or a layout, not in `blocks`.
- **Composition**: the automatic layout step that turns a slide's content into boxes with a position, size and font size. A `composition` key steers it.
- **Implicit blocks**: content fields written straight on the slide (`text`, `items`, `table`, ...) with no `blocks` list. Composition treats them as blocks, and editing can turn them into an explicit `blocks` list.
- **Promoted region**: content placed by a 3×3 grid key on the slide (`left`, `top:right`, `center+right`), used when relative position matters. It cannot be mixed with root content.
- **Catalog record**: one named, reusable design entry (a theme, layout, color scheme, font scheme, ...) from the `@openpresentation/gallery` package or a deck's `catalogs`. A deck refers to it by id.
- **Reflow**: text wrapping again when its box or size changes. OPF computes line breaks itself; PPTX export writes each wrapped line as its own text box, so text edited in PowerPoint or Keynote does not reflow.
- **Provenance**: tags that OPF writes into an exported PPTX to record where each shape came from, so a later import can map edits back to the OPF source.
<!-- glossary:end -->

## Choose the structure

- `blocks` with `composition.mode: auto` suit content that can reflow. Use `row`, `column`, or `grid` when arrangement matters; `columns` selects grid tracks or caps automatic candidates.
- `weights` size columns in row/grid/auto and rows in column mode. They describe relative allocation, not percentages or absolute pixels.
- Nested groups keep related content together. Each group contains `blocks` and optionally `composition`; it is not also a leaf `text`/`image` payload.
- Promoted regions (`left`, `center+right`, `top:left`, and the other schema-defined regions) preserve placement on a 3×3 vocabulary. Use disjoint regions; flow direction and weights do not relocate them.
- A layout record is one ordered list of `regions`, declared once, in reading order, with a `direction` (`row` side by side, `column` stacked) and a `size` per entry (a share of the parent; `auto` only in a column). An entry is a region (`name`, `accepts`, `flow`, ...) or a split (`direction` and its own `regions`, for two dimensions, three levels at most). `title` and `subtitle` are reserved heading regions that take only a size, their own `anchor` and (the title) `rule: true`, a short accent rule above a title with no tag; `anchor` on the record or a split is inherited by the body regions under it. Regions say which kinds they accept, how several blocks flow, and whether an empty region collapses. The 28 built-ins are named by intent (`cover`, `text`, `chart`, `two-column`, and others). Read `docs/layout-templates.md` and the actual record before pinning a block with `region`. Unpinned blocks bind by kind, role, then reading order; a pin names a region on that layout. Use `layout: "auto"` for explicit automatic composition. The slide's own groups remain content groups, not layout placeholders.

Read [geometry and repair](references/geometry.md) before measuring or paginating. The format schema is the source of numeric bounds; do not invent arbitrary `x`, `y`, `width`, or `fontSize` fields on leaf payloads.

## Match the structure to the content, and budget the slide

One idea per slide. Pick the layout from what the content is, then check it fits before you repair anything:

- Parallel entries with details: layout `pillars`, or labeled `items`. Figures that sit in prose: layout `metrics`. Options compared: layout `comparison` (two) or a `table`. A flow, system or hierarchy: a `diagram` block (Mermaid source) in a region that accepts `diagram` (the figure regions of the built-in layouts do, as does any region that takes every body kind); `process` draws its steps (one block each, or one list) under numbered badges on a connector, and a list of 3 to 6 one- or two-word steps as chips joined by arrows. `agenda` and `agenda-split` draw each part beside a numbered badge, so do not type numbers into the entries. Levels that build on each other (the few on top, the foundation at the base): layout `pyramid`, one list of 1 to 8 short entries, the top level first. Up to four parts of a whole, or the four cases of two choices: layout `matrix`, one list of 1 to 4 short entries (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right). Every entry is a node at one size, the list's role size or larger, never smaller: an entry too long for its node, a nested entry, a description, icons or numbering keep the list. Keep pyramid entries to a few words (the top level is a narrow triangle) and matrix entries to one or two short words a line. One message: layout `statement` (a sentence as the title over a short paragraph draws the title at display size; a metric, or a text under a short label title, is the message under a running head). A lone text of 25 words or fewer anywhere draws at display size, and the words you set bold in it draw in the primary color, so mark emphasis only where you mean it. A quote: layout `quote`. The opf-author skill's storyline guide has an example of each.
- A shape block (`shape` with no `image`, or `path`) is a figure too: it takes the largest frame of its preset's proportions in its cell (1:1 for round shapes, 2:1 for horizontal arrows, 3:2 for rectangles; `aspectRatio` overrides), centered, and a region that takes `image` takes shapes. Its text fits in the preset's text rectangle; a shape too small for its text reports `opf/text-overflow` at `<block>/text`, so give it a larger cell or fewer words.
- A diagram is a figure: composition centers it in its block (a region anchored to the bottom places it there), draws it at the largest label size that fits, from 32 reference pixels down, and never below the readability floor (`minFontSize`, 16 by default; the smallest secondary label stays at or above 75% of it). When it does not fit even there, it is drawn smaller and a `text-overflow` finding says so: give it a slide or a region of its own, beside short text rather than under long text, write it in the other direction (`flowchart TB` for `LR`), or split it into two diagrams. A `caption` is composed inside the block like an image's. A source that cannot be parsed draws a "Diagram unavailable" placeholder and reports its line and column.
- One block or group per slide can carry the takeaway on a contrasting card: `"emphasis": true` (a dark card on a light slide, an accent card on a dark one; colors from the scheme, text at 4.5:1). Do not mark more than one; a second is drawn plain. A short last text after longer blocks becomes a band at the foot automatically, and a very short one after a lone paragraph a pill under it (never at the cost of another block's size); `"emphasis": false` opts out. A scorecard's metrics are tiles: the value sized from the card, the label above it, the description and trend at the foot.
- A slide has a vertical budget. At the default sizes on a 16:9 slide, about 10 one-line list entries, 6 two-line entries, 6 entries with a description, 10 table rows or 4 paragraphs keep the engine's full text size; past that the whole block shrinks toward the 16 px floor and then overflows. For a talk, aim well under it (about 6 entries, 8 table rows, 3 paragraphs). The [vertical budget](references/geometry.md#vertical-budget) lists the measured figures and the `composeSlide` call that measures your own content; label them as estimates without a font measurement.
- Without a layout, one picture among other blocks takes a full-height, cover-cropped side track, and one chart whose plot would be too short takes a 3:2 track beside the other blocks (`geometry.track`). Give a screenshot or diagram that must stay whole `fit: "contain"`; it then flows in the grid.
- When the budget is exceeded, split the slide where the story breaks (two ideas, two slides), or run `opf paginate deck.opf.json --check` and then `opf paginate deck.opf.json -i` to let the engine split what does not fit. Do not lower `minFontSize` or shorten the content to make it pass.

## Headers and footers

Header and footer zones (`left`, `center`, `right`) sit on the content inset, so a footer's left edge meets the body's. They are three columns with a small gutter, and a zone beside empty zones takes their width: a lone `left` footer spans the content width, and a `left` and a `right` zone share the free center. A `center` zone stays centered. A zone that still wraps onto more than two lines gets the `opf/furniture-wraps` warning: shorten it, or move a source to a `caption` on the chart, table or image it describes, or to the speaker notes. See `docs/dynamic-composition.md` for the geometry (`furniture-flow-v2`).

## Repair loop

Inspect the current slide, effective dimensions, resolved layout, fonts, and path-specific diagnostics. Give content more space, change the grouping or arrangement, or split it explicitly. Summarize or remove content only when the user's request permits it. Reducing `minFontSize` below readability is not a successful repair.

Use the same font measurement provider and resolved design for preview, layout, and export. Without actual font measurements, label results as estimates. `overflow: error` gates supported text fit; it does not certify complex charts, images, or PowerPoint pixel parity.

Pagination is an explicit authoring operation. Review the returned ordinary slides and source mappings, then validate and render them. A renderer should not secretly export a different paginated document from the one shown in the editor. The one exception is the export's continuation (LH-90): `opf convert` and `convert` draw an overfull slide of sibling blocks of one kind (eight timelines, each with any note after it) as several slides, split between whole blocks, and report it as `slide-continued` with the report field `continued`. Treat that finding like an overflow: split the slide in the deck where the story breaks, or run `opf paginate`, so the deck and the editor show the same slides; `--keep-slides` draws it as written. The player, `<opf-deck>` and the editor show the same pages (LH-99: steps after the slide, or a stacked thumbnail and a read-only page preview in the editor) without changing the deck or its slide numbers, so the deck as written still overflows until you split it. An unsplittable item requires a targeted change; do not ignore a pagination error or use partial output.
